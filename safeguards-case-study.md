# Safeguards Case Study
### Reading a real single-user AI-assisted project through the lens of AI Safeguards — and projecting it to adversarial scale

## Elevator Pitch

Safeguards that protect a single-user application—human approval gates, automated checks, audit trails—may degrade or fail when scaled to adversarial populations. This case study examines a real AI-assisted project built with such mechanisms from inception: deny-by-default governance, quality checkpoints across agent workflows, and traceability hooks that caught policy violations in supervised operation. We trace each safeguard from single-founder oversight to population-scale pressure, exposing both the principles that hold under adversarial stress and the architectural fractures that emerge only in production. The central question is whether the mechanisms designed for one trusted supervisor can survive when millions of users, thousands of concurrent agents, and bad actors all probe the same checkpoints simultaneously.

## Context: The System Under Study

### The Vitalis Project

Vitalis (formerly CalmKit) is a single-founder software initiative launched by Andrés with zero capital. Its goal is to build a family of free Progressive Web Applications (PWAs) that address wellness challenges—anxiety, sleep, burnout, digestion—initially targeting organic growth via search engine optimization and monetization through Google AdSense and affiliate marketing. The technical stack is deliberately constrained: vanilla HTML5, CSS3, and ES6+ JavaScript with no external frameworks, Service Workers for offline functionality, browser-native storage (localStorage), and free hosting via Netlify or Vercel. The ambition is to scale from a solo founder to a sustainable business serving millions of users without venture capital, leveraging AI assistance to multiply productivity.

The system's safeguard relevance stems from its wellness domain. Tools generated by Vitalis handle mental health and physical wellbeing content—areas where erroneous advice or unsupervised AI output could harm users. Vitalis is explicitly built on the principle that such tools require comprehensive disclaimers, legal review, and human approval before reaching any audience. The project's governing charter emphasizes a core commitment: to honor life by caring for it and protecting it without causing harm, and to ensure that any tool which could be misread as substitute medical or psychological advice carries all legally required disclaimers, with Andrés consulting and approving the form before publication.

### The Multi-Agent Working Method

Rather than rely on a single AI model, Vitalis adopts a collaborative division-of-labor approach across multiple large language model instances. The method assigns distinct roles to models suited to each role: **Opus** acts as project director and final evaluator, synthesizing proposals and architectural decisions; **Sonnet** serves as the quality guardian, reviewing work for consistency, accuracy, and alignment with constraints; **Haiku** drafts atomic, modular pieces of work—"fichas de encargo" (work orders)—each with explicit boundaries of what must be included and what must be avoided.

Critically, this is not autonomous multi-agent reasoning. Every output is subject to human approval. Andrés reviews all substantive decisions before any code is written, deployed, or published. A proposal must pass human inspection and receive explicit approval to proceed. Between sessions, a persistent file-based memory system (git-tracked markdown files) preserves prior decisions, constraints, architectural choices, and lessons learned, ensuring continuity across separate conversations.

### The ASLAN-ARC Dashboard

In parallel, Andrés maintains ASLAN-ARC, a multi-project monitoring dashboard styled as an "Iron Man HUD"—a real-time browser-based view aggregating status and metrics across multiple concurrent projects. Built in vanilla JavaScript with Web Components (no dependencies), the dashboard enforces two non-negotiable principles: **data honesty** (metrics and state reflect reality; no fabrication of data to show false progress) and **automated enforcement**. An audit hook inspects edits to the dashboard's core `.js` files and blocks any edit that violates the project's `file://` architectural invariants (e.g., patterns involving `import`, `export`, `type="module"`, or `fetch()`); this hook has fired on real edits in practice. The dashboard maintains comprehensive telemetry and audit logging, creating a persistent record of what changed, when, and by whom—essential for reviewing whether safeguard boundaries held under operational stress.

### The User and What Is Protected

The "user" or supervisor in this system is singular: Andrés, the founder. What the safeguards protect includes: (1) **codebase integrity**—ensuring that code changes align with the project's architecture and constraints; (2) **human decision authority**—enforcing that no AI system unilaterally commits changes, makes strategic pivots, or publishes content without Andrés's explicit prior consent; and (3) **end-user wellbeing**—ensuring that any wellness tool reaching eventual users includes appropriate legal safeguards, medical disclaimers, and has undergone human review for potential to cause harm.

This is a constrained, supervised environment: one human, known governance boundaries, explicit approval gates, and persistent audit trails. The case study will examine how this structure—designed for a single founder and small AI-assisted team—scales (or fails to scale) when the system moves toward production with adversarial populations, untrusted operators, or distributed autonomous agents.
