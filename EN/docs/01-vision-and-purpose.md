## 1. Vision and purpose

Collaborating with AI without structure produces three almost inevitable problems: **loss of control** over what the AI does and why, **quality degradation** because there are no clear review criteria, and **broken continuity** when the memory of the previous conversation is lost or misread.

**The central thesis is simple: each AI model has distinct strengths, valuable in their own context. We assign each stage of a job to the model best suited to it, while a human acts as the human authority who approves, rejects or improves before anything is executed.** A model that excels at direction is not the best at detailed drafting; a critical reviewer is not the best at fast ideation. Persistent memory across sessions ties up the loose ends: prior decisions, accumulated context, lessons learned. This method is not theory: it came out of applying it on real projects of my own, where structure made the difference between chaos and clarity.

The result is a **reproducible method**: anyone with access to different AI models and these basic rules can replicate what works, adapting it to their own project. It is not magic, nor "autonomous" AI; it is the opposite: maximum clarity about who decides what, maximum human control, maximum specialization of roles.

Why does this matter more with AI than with a traditional human team? Because AI models are **disposable and fast** — you can switch models, restart sessions, delegate without friction. A human team requires established trust and slow-built context; AI requires the opposite: explicit structure and agreements about limits. Without that structure, the result is chaotic. With it, it is predictable.

This method rests on three pillars: **clearly defined roles**, **a reproducible cycle**, and **memory continuity**. Together they make working with AI something another human — or the same human in a different session — can understand, audit and run with no surprises. AI augments human judgment with information, analysis and the generation of options; the human keeps authority over every decision that matters. It is collaboration, not automation.
